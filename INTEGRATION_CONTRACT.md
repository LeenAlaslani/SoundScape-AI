# Integration contract — for the audio teammate

Vision, audio and frontend can be built in parallel if the following protocol stays stable.

## Vision → audio payload

```json
{
  "scene": "forest",
  "sound": "rain",
  "intensity": "gentle",
  "comfort_mode": true,
  "seconds": 10
}
```

- `scene`: forest | coast | desert | river | waterfall | mountain | lake | unknown
- `sound`: rain | sea_waves | wind | pouring_water | thunderstorm
- `intensity`: gentle | moderate | strong (requested **character** of the audio, not the volume)
- `comfort_mode`: true | false (extra prompt instruction, **not** evidence of tested subjective comfort)
- `seconds`: integer from 5 to 30

POST `/api/compose` accepts that payload and returns an English `prompt` and a `negative_prompt`. At this stage, the endpoint **does not invoke** the audio generator; it only constructs the description.

The app's `SoundSettings` and prompt generator live in `app/prompting.py`. If the teammate adds additional intensity labels, update the enum with explicit review; avoid silent mismatches.

## Five existing WAV assets

The prior notebook produces five 60-second WAVs at **16,000 Hz**, names shown in `assets/audio/README.md`.

Use `/api/mix` with the sound-layer controls independently of `/api/compose`. Mixing amplitude by sliders **is not** controllable generation and should not be reported as evidence of the audio fine-tuning task.

## Evaluation handoff

The audio teammate should send: their `.ipynb` including the EDA and training cells, exact pretrained checkpoint ID, train/validation/test split, dataset source/license, generated examples before and after fine-tuning, listening evaluation notes, inference code, package versions and output sample rate. Do not claim fine-tuning improved sound quality until measured.
